Observe your svara
Swar Yoga · Shiva Swarodaya

Your breath changes sides all day. Nobody has measured it properly.

The old texts say the active nostril turns roughly every hour, and that the turning follows the lunar day. Modern physiology calls the same thing the nasal cycle and still argues about what drives it. We're asking practitioners to log theirs, and we'll publish what the record shows — including if it disagrees with the texts.

When and where

The svara cycle is counted from sunrise, and the rule depends on the lunar day — so the tool needs your date, time and rough position. Correct anything that looks wrong.

Your position is used in this browser to work out sunrise. It is not sent anywhere and is not stored.

What this tool does and doesn't do

Three different kinds of statement get mixed together in most breath apps. We keep them apart.

Your observation

What you personally report after sitting and noticing.

Traditional expectation

What the Svarodaya rules say should be flowing, with the rule shown.

Physiological measurement

Nothing here. A webcam cannot measure nasal airflow, and this tool does not pretend otherwise.

Sensor mode is reserved for later. A bilateral nasal airflow sensor could eventually provide a physiological measurement instead of a self-reported observation. Until one is connected, no sensor values are shown — real or simulated.

    The three swaras

    What the texts say each side is for

    Traditional guidance, offered as tradition. It's exactly what we're testing — treat it as a hypothesis, not a prescription.

    Ida · left

    Chandra swara — cooling
    • Study, writing, planning
    • Meditation and long sitting
    • Water, gentle food
    • Setting out on a journey

    Pingala · right

    Surya swara — heating
    • Physical work and exercise
    • Digestion, the heavier meal
    • Tasks needing drive
    • Difficult conversations

    Sushumna · both

    The turning point
    • Brief — often minutes
    • Reserved for sitting

    The questions we're actually asking

    Stated up front, so the answers mean something

    1. Does the turn really take about an hour? Or does it vary far more than the texts allow?
    2. Does the tithi predict the sunrise swara? Testable, and either answer is worth printing.
    3. Does the active side track energy and mood? Self-reported, so treat it gently.
    4. Does regular practice change the pattern? Comparing course students with everyone else.
    Your data, plainly

    What we collect, and what we never will

    We ask for the least that makes the research possible. You can download everything we hold on you, or delete all of it, from your account page — no email required, no reason needed.

    We collect

    Nostril side, timestamp, city-level location, and — only if you choose — a mood and energy score.

    We never

    Sell it, share it with advertisers, track you across other sites, or collect anything from under-18s.
