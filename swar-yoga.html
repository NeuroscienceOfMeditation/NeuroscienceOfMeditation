Observe your svara
Swar Yoga · Shiva Swarodaya

Your breath changes sides all day. Nobody has measured it properly.

The old texts say the active nostril turns roughly every hour, and that the turning follows the lunar day. Modern physiology calls the same thing the nasal cycle and still argues about what drives it. We're asking practitioners to log theirs, and we'll publish what the record shows — including if it disagrees with the texts.

Not sure? Check it against a mirror

Breathe out onto a cool mirror, or the back of your hand, held just below the nostrils. Compare the two patches of condensation — the larger one marks the open side. Exhaled air is saturated at body temperature and condenses in a pattern set by the geometry of the nasal jet, which changes with congestion.

darpaṇena samālokya tatra śvāsaṃ vinikṣipet

nirīkṣitavyaṃ yatnena sadā pratyūṣakālataḥ

It should be examined carefully every day from daybreak. Verse 149 — the one instruction the source calls the most important practical content in the book, and it is graded E. Waking is the day's largest routine autonomic transition, and a habit anchored to an unavoidable daily event is the only kind that lasts.

One minute, before rising, before any device. Which side is flowing, the rough rate and depth of the breath, one word for the state. Thirty mornings establish a baseline; sixty reveal structure.

    How this tool works, and what it will not tell you
    Your observation

    What you report after sitting and noticing, plus anything you measure with a mirror or the back of your hand.

    Traditional expectation

    What the Svarodaya rules predict, with the verse and grade shown for each rule used.

    Physiological measurement

    None from the camera. A webcam cannot measure nasal airflow, so this tool does not claim to. Sensor mode is reserved for real hardware.

    The tool will not tell you to refrain from acting. Verse 64 instructs abstention when observation contradicts expectation, and that rule is unfalsifiable by construction — it reinterprets its own failures as inauspicious conditions, so it can never be wrong. It is shown in the result panel and never acted on. This is a contemplative practice, not medical diagnosis or treatment.

    The three swaras

    What the texts say each side is for

    Traditional guidance, offered as tradition. It's exactly what we're testing — treat it as a hypothesis, not a prescription.

    Ida · left

    Chandra swara — cooling
    • Study, writing, planning
    • Meditation and long sitting
    • Water, gentle food
    • Setting out on a journey

    Pingala · right

    Surya swara — heating
    • Physical work and exercise
    • Digestion, the heavier meal
    • Tasks needing drive
    • Difficult conversations

    Sushumna · both

    The turning point
    • Brief — often minutes
    • Reserved for sitting
    • Not for worldly business
    • Marks the change of side

    The questions we're actually asking

    Stated up front, so the answers mean something

    1. Does the turn really take about an hour? Or does it vary far more than the texts allow?
    2. Does the tithi predict the sunrise swara? Testable, and either answer is worth printing.
    3. Does the active side track energy and mood? Self-reported, so treat it gently.
    4. Does regular practice change the pattern? Comparing course students with everyone else.
