Many non-dual philosophies treat the world as a kind of mistake: see through it
and you're free. Abhinavagupta, writing in Kashmir a thousand years ago, said
something stranger. The world isn't a mistake. It's what a free, self-aware
consciousness looks like when it expresses itself. What binds us isn't the world
but a narrowed sense of who is seeing it.

The new essay goes through the ideas that hold his system together:

- light and self-awareness (*prakāśa* and *vimarśa*)
- freedom (*svātantrya*), and how the world appears
- how the infinite "contracts" into a person, through the tattvas, kañcukas and malas
- liberation as recognition (*pratyabhijñā*) of something that was never lost

There's also a section on his theory of *rasa*, on why a tragedy on stage moves
us without hurting us, and a side-by-side table with Śaṅkara's Advaita Vedānta.
